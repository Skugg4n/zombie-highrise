# ZOMBIE HIGH RISE - Art direction

## Keywords

**Zombies in daylight.** Warm, sunny, almost idyllic outdoors. The contrast
is the point: the threat looks almost comical at 200 meters in sunshine and
becomes unsettling only up close, or when you are inside something tight.

The style is **stylized realism-light**: low polygon counts with clean
silhouettes, but PBR-ish lighting, soft haze and deliberate color work.
Think less "cartoon", more "diorama with real light".

The world outside is a **wasteland**: sun-bleached, half-ruined, quietly
beautiful in a Fallout kind of way (tone reference only). Gear looks
scavenged and improvised: taped grips, mismatched parts, worn edges.
Visual references for the critics are listed in docs/vision.md.

## Three worlds

1. **Ground level / day:** golden warm light, light haze, saturated but not
   neon colors. Long sightlines out of the fortified base: roads, fields,
   hills. Dust in the air. This is the "breathe out" level type.
2. **Upper floors:** interiors lit by windows, balcony views down at the
   street, verticality. Halfway between open and closed.
3. **Basement / trenches / night:** cold and sparse light, the flashlight
   cone is the main light. Deliberately claustrophobic. Sound does half the
   work here.

Night waves are lit by the building's own lamps, flares and muzzle flashes,
never pure black. The player must always be able to read the scene.

## Palette

- Day: warm sand, sun-bleached wood, sky blue, yellow-green grass.
- Night: deep blue (not black), warm glow points (lamps, flares), cold flashlight.
- Zombies: pale gray with one clear accent color readable at long range.
- Enemy readability beats realism: silhouette and color must identify an
  enemy type in a tenth of a second.

## The quality bar

The critic agents' benchmark: screenshots must hold up next to modern
stylized commercial titles in the genre and feel deliberately designed,
never like programmer art. Every photo mode view must have: a clear focal
point, depth in three layers (foreground, midground, background),
intentional lighting, and no surface that looks unfinished.

Quest 2 sets the technical ceiling, so beauty must come from light, color,
composition and atmosphere, not texture resolution or polygon counts.

## Details that sell the illusion

- Dust motes in sun rays
- Wind in grass and fabric (cheap vertex animation)
- Blob shadows under everything that moves
- Muzzle flashes briefly lighting the surroundings
- The elevator's worn metal, flickering fluorescent light, buttons you
  physically press in VR
