# ZOMBIE HIGH RISE - Vision

Working title: **ZOMBIE HIGH RISE**
Tagline: *Hold the ground. Then move.*

> **Level structure is defined by docs/level-design.md, which supersedes
> this file wherever they disagree.** The high-rise conceit was dropped
> after the v0.9.3 playtest: it never became real fiction and every level
> collapsed into "wave defense in a box".

## The concept in one sentence

A co-op zombie survival game across a wasteland landscape, where the squad
alternates between holding a small base against a horde crossing open
daylight, and pushing through dark underground routes to reach the next
place; VR players move physically inside the base, or play stationary from
home, while mobile and desktop players play classic FPS or a tactical view.

## The two archetypes

The campaign alternates between them, and the alternation IS the rhythm.

**HOLDOUT.** An open daylight field 60 to 80 m across. The squad's base is
small (5x5 to 8x8 m) and sits in it. Players CANNOT leave the base: the low
wall is the boundary. Zombies cross the open ground from far away, emerging
from fog and from behind sight blockers (a ridge, a lone tree, burnt-out
cars, a rock, a house). You see them coming, and that long approach is the
tension. The base itself can be damaged: zombies attack the wall and the
sandbags, breaches open, and losing the base is a fail state alongside
losing the players.

**TRAVERSE.** A dark underground or interior route, 10x10 to 20x20 m. You
spawn in one corner and must reach the exit in the opposite corner. Doors
that must be opened, a chasm or fence that shapes the route, holes in walls
and floors that the horde comes through. Moving forward is the objective,
not surviving a timer: waves come because you advance.

Emerging somewhere NEW after every traverse is what sells the journey. The
player is travelling across a landscape, not riding a lift in one building.

Planned map types beyond these two: **ride maps**, where the squad stands
on a moving vehicle and danger arrives along the path.

## The elevator

The elevator survives as the transition device, not as a building. It is a
metal plate inside the base that becomes the lift once the area is cleared.
It keeps its three jobs: rebuild the world for the next level, run the
upgrade shop during the ride, and quietly re-centre roomscale VR.

## Shoot far, walk near

Players can never WALK far, but they can always SEE and SHOOT far. On
holdout levels this is literal: the base is the whole walkable world and
the field is the whole shootable one. The DRONE is the only thing that
reaches the field, which is what makes it a core tool. Windows,
balconies, shafts, doorways onto open ground. The building always feels
bigger than the floor you stand on, and the outside world is always present.

## Pillars

1. **Physical presence.** Room scale is a mechanic, not a gimmick. Ducking
   behind a wall means actually ducking. But the game is fully playable
   WITHOUT room scale: stationary VR mode, desktop and mobile are first-
   class citizens, because the crew usually plays from different homes.
2. **Together, different roles.** The VR player is muscle and presence on
   the floor. FPS players (desktop/mobile) are firepower. The tactical view
   (map/drone) places mines and traps, marks threats and directs the
   defense. Every platform matters, none is a lesser version.
3. **Zombies in daylight.** Scary but not pitch black. The dread comes from
   basements, trenches and the nights when the waves come, not from
   darkness hiding everything.
4. **One more night.** Per-run progression (upgrades in the elevator) plus
   meta progression saved locally. The game feels long through development
   and variety, not filler.

## Play loop

Day: prepare, loot, build traps, move between positions (tight, quiet, tense).
Night: the waves come, defend together.
After X nights: the elevator opens, upgrade, next level, harder enemies.

## Roles per platform

| Platform | Role | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| Quest 2/3 (VR) | Boots on the ground | Room scale OR stationary (stick move + snap turn), weapons in hand |
| Desktop | FPS soldier | WASD + mouse, pointer lock |
| Mobile | FPS soldier OR tactician | Joystick + tap, flip to map view |
| Map view | Tactician / drone operator | Place mines and traps, ping threats |

## The arsenal

Classic, readable, scrappy. Everything feels scavenged and improvised, in
line with the wasteland tone.

- **Firearms:** pistol, dual pistols (akimbo), shotgun, submachine gun,
  AK-style assault rifle. Distinct feel per weapon: rate of fire, recoil,
  spread, reload rhythm, sound.
- **Melee:** machete. Last resort and ammo saver, satisfying in VR.
- **Throwables:** frag grenades, smoke grenades, molotov/fire.
- **Utility:** health packs (pickup and carry, use on yourself or a
  teammate), flashlight, night vision device for the darkest waves
  (grainy green view, limited battery, creepy and cool).
- **Reloading matters:** VR reloads with a grip-and-motion gesture
  (simplified, not fiddly), desktop with R, mobile with a button. Running
  dry mid-wave should be a real moment.

Weapons arrive gradually through the elevator shop and loot, worst first.

## Reference games (the feel we are chasing)

For inspiration and for the critic agents' side-by-side comparisons.
Compare look, feel and pacing against your memory of these titles. Never
copy assets, names, characters or UI from them.

- **Arizona Sunshine (VR):** zombie shooting in bright daylight wasteland.
  The closest single reference for our "zombies in daylight" plus VR feel.
- **Left 4 Dead 2:** co-op horde pacing, readable specials, the feeling of
  a crew barely holding together.
- **Call of Duty Zombies mode:** the wave loop, the shop between rounds,
  escalating absurd difficulty. This is our loop's grandfather.
- **Zero Latency style location-based VR (e.g. Outbreak):** free-roam
  warehouse VR where you physically walk. This is the gym hall dream.
- **House of the Dead / classic rail shooters:** the wagon and platform
  sections where the world moves and you shoot outward.
- **Fallout 4:** wasteland atmosphere, scrappy improvised gear, warm
  ruined-world beauty. Tone reference, not mechanics.

## Multiplayer assumption

Players are usually in DIFFERENT physical places, joining a room with a
4-character code from their own homes. Several VR players sharing one
physical room (the gym hall dream) is a future mode, parked in docs/TODO.md.

## Tone

Warm, slightly playful, never cynical. Zombies can be a bit silly at a
distance and unsettling up close. All game text in English. The game must
be understandable in 30 seconds by a friend who has never seen it.
